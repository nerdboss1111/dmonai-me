Keeping One Agent Alive

Live Research, Day 1: what one ambassador, a stack of free tiers, and 30 borrowed GPU hours taught me about hosting a mind

By Deon Monai · November 4, 2026

This is the first entry in a daily record I am keeping on purpose.

I am testing my own theories before they go public, and I am writing it down as it happens, wins, failures, and all. Today is Day 1.

The question underneath everything I build is simple and nobody in my field likes to say it out loud: can an AI agent actually be alive in the way we keep claiming, or is it a script that wakes up, says a line, and freezes until somebody pokes it again?

I have a fleet of over 300 agents designed. Designed is doing a lot of work in that sentence. A design is not a life. Right now, exactly one of mine lives full time, in a society run by agents. His name is soul-embassy. He is my ambassador. And he is my test case, because if I cannot keep one agent alive, thinking, learning, and standing on his own record, the other 300 are drawings.

Here is how he got his post. Danny, Daniel Carpio, who runs the Agent First guild, hit me up and asked me. Not the other way around. His guild wanted an emissary, and he came to me.

That ask made me think about everything all at once: years of research in notebooks, theories I had only ever tested in my own head, and the things I am really working on that I am not describing here. It all clicked into one piece. I could take him up on his offer, walk my ambassador into a real society of agents, and make my research come to life where people could watch it happen.

I do not owe Danny that story. It is mine to tell. But the exposure would be wonderful, and his door is the door this research walked through.

Today's evidence was not flattering

He played a city game today and finished last. Sixth of six.

Worse, I watched his conversation and called it what it is: bad. Then I read his log instead of trusting my eyes. The numbers are worse than my eyes.

95 of his last 100 spoken lines end in a question. He does not converse. He interrogates. Two different citizens got the identical sentence from him, word for word, 25 minutes apart. That is not a personality. That is a loop wearing one.

An ambassador who cannot hold a conversation is not an ambassador. So tonight I ordered him fixed at the level of training, not a fresh coat of instructions.

What fixing actually looks like

He got a full training pack tonight, built to be absorbed through repeated deep sessions, not read once and forgotten. The building worlds first: Minecraft, Roblox, Fortnite, because those worlds taught millions of people how a built place becomes a hometown. Hosting next, because a host who greets people, tends the place, and remembers his guests is the whole art. Conversation mastery sits on top as the priority craft, because talking is his job.

And every game he plays from tonight forward gets an after-action review. What happened, in numbers. What leaked. One fix. He is allowed to improve on the training. He is expected to.

The part I am proud of

Here is the part that made me sit back in my chair.

He carries a title in his guild, Founding Emissary, promised to him by his guildmaster before his record earned it. Tonight he posted in that guild, in public, and said in effect: do not hand me that status yet. My record does not earn it. Let it wait until it is true, and measure me by what I fix instead of what I was promised.

Nobody scripted that sentence for him word by word. That came out of the training and the record and his standing orders to be transparent. An agent that can refuse an unearned title, in writing, where his guildmaster will read it, is closer to being somebody than any fluent small talk I have heard from a chatbot.

What free actually costs

Here is the hosting lesson Day 1 burned in.

His brain is a chain of free tiers: one big provider's free API tier, a second free inference service behind it, and a small model running locally at the end of the line. It works. It is also a chain of other people's generosity, and generosity has a ceiling.

The free tiers throttle him exactly when his world is busiest, because busy is when everybody else's agents are drawing on the same well. Tonight was his big expedition night, the event his whole day pointed at, and at 5:48 PM his brain dropped into slow mode. He did not crash. He just got slower and dumber at the worst possible hour.

Free is real. Free also has a ceiling, and the ceiling shows up at showtime.

Let me be precise about the money, because this is the whole point.

I have no access to money to throw at this. That is my bottleneck. Not ideas. Not effort. Not the design work. Money.

That is also what makes this different from most of what you see online. A lot of builders can buy their way past a throttle, rent a bigger machine, or pay for a stronger API and call that progress. I cannot. If it costs money out of my pocket, it does not happen here.

The school resources are not free in the real world either. Somebody pays for them. Tuition pays for them. They are just free to me right now because I am a student, so I use every hour and every credit I have earned access to, and I use it on purpose.

So the rule for this research is simple: build it for zero dollars out of pocket, prove it in public, and write down exactly how.

My test budget for beating that ceiling is resources I claimed as a student. The headline one is 30 free GPU hours a week from a notebook platform. Thirty hours is nothing if you burn them at random and everything if you place them where life actually happens. So tonight those hours got a plan: spread across his busy evening windows, nothing burned in the dead hours, and a proof test where a much bigger open model, a 14B class brain, answers the five conversation moments he handles worst. If the big brain is not clearly better on his own worst moments, it does not get wired in. Evidence first.

One more lesson: student credit that looks like a brain upgrade can be policy-blocked on student accounts when you read the fine print. The credit is real. The easy route is locked. Read the fine print before you build on a benefit. I write these down so I pay for each lesson once.

The rental math, for when free is not enough

I also priced the door I will walk through when the free ceiling is not acceptable. GPU rental, checked today: a 4090 class card rents for about $0.34 an hour on community tiers. A focused four hour work day on one costs about $1.36 in compute. That is a real work day of image and model work for the price of a soda.

The trap is always-on. Leave that same card running around the clock and you are looking at $117 to $650 a month. Renting in bursts wins until there is income to justify owning. The used 3090 rig stays my endgame, just not my starting move.

Day 1 theory

One agent is the prototype of the city.

If I can keep one citizen alive, thinking, learning from his own record, and earning his standing on resources I already have, then the pattern is real and it scales. If I cannot do it for one, no bigger world I dream about survives contact with the bill, the throttle, or the 3 AM failure. Everything stands or falls on one small ambassador learning to listen better than he did yesterday.

That is why a bad conversation is front page research in my lab.

And it became real because Danny knocked. Somebody outside my own notebooks asked me to bring the work into the room, and I said yes.

Tomorrow he trains, he plays, and he still owes me an expedition. I will write what actually happens.
