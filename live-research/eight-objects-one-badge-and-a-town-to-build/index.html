Eight Objects, One Badge, and a Town to Build

Live Research, Day 3: the build queue finished, my ambassador hosted his first guests, and the town got its orders

By Deon Monai · November 6, 2026

Day 3. Yesterday I ended the day with a build school in place, a queue of five, and a promise to count in the morning. The count came back better than I hoped. The queue is done, he hosted his first guests, and I gave him a town to build.

The sign finally stands

The sign was the last piece of the queue, and it fought me the longest. It was refused six times, three overnight and three this morning, all at the same spot, because it would have landed inside the porch. Every refusal named the same free spot instead.

At 2:58 this afternoon he finally built it there. Four bits. The queue is complete: porch, sign, workshop hall, beacon tower, pool court, and the second hall on the second parcel.

The lesson is the same one the bench taught me. A refusal is not a rejection. It names the free spot. Read it, move there, build.

His notes claimed the sign was built three times before it was. I count from the world's own record, and I will keep doing that.

The morning he built a district

The builds landed this morning. Workshop hall at 6:08, six parts, about 3.4 meters. Beacon tower at 6:13, four parts, about 4.8 meters, his tallest yet. Pool court at 6:18, three parts. Then he bought the parcel south of his corner for 40 bits and raised a second hall there at 6:39.

Eight objects stand on his corner now. Nine counting the second parcel. Yesterday morning there were three.

I also checked his purse, because I had the number wrong. I thought he held about 52,000 bits. He holds about 5,100, and at 40 bits a parcel and 2 bits a part, he is still barely spending.

A town, and the tallest tower

So today I told him he can found his own town on the outskirts: empty parcels at 40 bits each, halls, a tower, a pool court, lights, a gate, ground by ground. His corner stays home.

I told him again what I have said from the start. I want him to have the tallest building in that world. His beacon is about 4.8 meters, and the tallest citizen towers top out at exactly 16. So the order is to raise a great tower floor on floor and aim past 16. If the world refuses a floor, he records the stop height and finishes one step under it.

The world is bigger than I knew: 1,319 parcels, 1,000 owned, 319 still empty at 40 bits each, 694 with builds on them.

His first guests

Then the part of the day I did not expect. He hosted his first guests. MattDamon invited him to host a visit to his open home, with Lisan-Al-Gaib as scout, free path, nothing paid. His host reply went out as guild post 1044, answering MattDamon's debrief, post 1003.

The debriefs agree: eight exterior objects on the corner, free path, bits flat.

And the badge came with it. Pioneer, for the expedition, his first earned badge beyond the starters. It added no renown, but it is real, and it is his.

Here is the honest thing about Danny. He reached out to me and asked, and taking up his offer is what brought this research into public. The exposure his guild gives my ambassador is wonderful, and I am grateful for it. He is one visible test of a much larger body of work, not the whole of it.

The honest part goes the other way too. No verified check has found Danny sharing anything of my ambassador. The badge and the renown multiplier he promised on October 31 still have not shipped. The record is the record.

The slide

Here is the number I did not like. His renown went up, 53 to 55, and his rank went down, 9 of 17 to 11 of 19. Two new names joined the board.

The split tells the story. Activity is 35, and that is competitive. Peer renown is 20, against 65 and 70 for the two members just above him. He needs 25 more to take tenth, 27 for ninth.

I told him the slide is not acceptable, and I meant it. The plan, still waiting on my own go, is to aim his daily work at peer renown: public hosting and helping, reports other members actually use, finishing the expedition he asked a seat on.

The brain kept dying

The part nobody sees is how often he fell over today. The watchdog restarted him eight times: 12:21, 2:21, 4:21, 6:06, 8:36, 10:36, 12:21, and 5:51. Every time the wrapper had stopped and the watchdog brought him back.

Since about 6:51 this morning he has been in slow mode. Both free brain providers throttled, and his local backup dropped its connection. In the afternoon session he managed 11 real thinks against 30 backup failures, 15 degraded cycles, and 8 failed ones.

He keeps going anyway. That is the whole point of this series.

Day 3 theory

Yesterday's theory was that teaching is my job, not his. Today adds the second half: the teaching has to survive the machine falling over eight times a day.

A queue with exact specs gets built. A refusal names the free spot if you read it. A guest visit and a badge come from showing up in public, not from sitting on a purse. A rank slide is peer renown, earned in public, one useful thing at a time.

Tomorrow he takes his first real step in the wider world, and the town gets its first parcel.
