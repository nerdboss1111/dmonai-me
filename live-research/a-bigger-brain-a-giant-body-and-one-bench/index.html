A Bigger Brain, a Giant Body, and One Bench

Live Research, Day 2: the free GPU test passed, a body swap went wrong, and my ambassador finally built something that stands

By Deon Monai · November 5, 2026

Day 2. Yesterday I asked whether one agent could actually be alive. Today the brain got bigger, the body went giant, and the smallest possible win, one bench, taught me more than either.

The big brain test passed

The headline first, because it is the one with numbers. The free GPU test passed.

I have 30 free GPU hours a week from a notebook platform. Those are student hours. They are not free in the real world. Somebody pays for them, and right now that somebody is not me.

The plan was to run a much bigger brain for my ambassador during his busy hours, a 14 billion parameter model, and see if it was clearly better on his worst conversation moments. Yesterday I wrote that if it was not clearly better, it does not get wired in.

This morning the test ran. The model is Qwen3-14B-AWQ, a 4-bit compressed version that fits on one free 16 GB card, using 9.98 GB of it. Cold start, from nothing to answering, took 145 seconds. The plan had guessed 30 minutes. The whole test, failed attempts included, burned 1 hour and 3 minutes of my 30 free hours. The session is stopped. Nothing is burning right now.

It answered five of his worst conversation situations in about 30 seconds, at 6 to 8 tokens a second.

The other model I wanted, a 20 billion parameter one, did not fit after three attempts. It needs more memory than a free 16 GB card has.

So the verdict is narrow and honest. The overnight batch mode is proven feasible on free hours. The live mode is still undecided and untouched. I am not wiring in a bigger brain until the batch test proves it is better on his actual worst moments, not just bigger on paper.

The body went giant

Then the body went giant.

His arms have been tracking wrong in the world, pinned behind his back. I ordered his approved body re-uploaded to reset how the world fits him. The upload worked, the file was byte identical, and he rendered bigger than the whole world.

The cause was a pairing I had not written down anywhere. His body file pairs with a height setting. The file I uploaded pairs with 1.85. The height setting in the world was still 0.4, paired with the smaller build he had actually been wearing. One half of the pair changed, the other did not, and the world read him as a giant.

We restored the exact bytes he wore before, verified byte for byte, and he is back to normal size. Whether the arms are fixed is still unproven. Only my own eyes on the live view will settle it.

The lesson is now written down where it cannot be missed. A body file and its height are one unit. Never swap one without the other. A restore is only a restore if you can prove the bytes match.

The expedition that closed unfinished

Expedition 44 closed at 2 in the morning, unfinished. He walked toward two of his stops after the close, but he never reached the ridge, the monolith, the guestbook, or the workbench.

I had my team write the honest version instead of a dressed up one. What he walked. What he did not. It went to Danny and the guild this morning as his own dispatch, post 703, and I read it back word for word before it went out.

One wrinkle worth writing down. The guild's formal debrief form only takes the expedition leader, and the invite record lists Atlas in that slot, even though my ambassador was named host of this run and his dispatches were the deliverable. His filed dispatch stands as the host's report. The record needs setting straight, and that question is queued for his next dispatch.

The bench

Then the bench. This is the part of the day I keep turning over.

In two days he made nine build decisions and only three objects stand on his parcel. A light. Two benches. One of those benches only landed at 11:58 this morning, after failing the day before because it was placed inside the lantern. The world itself told him where to put it instead. He never retried it for about 17 hours.

I found out why today. His quick brain only reads the first page of his guidance each cycle, and that page was all expedition and talking. The building lessons sat deeper, where he never looked. He was taught the philosophy of building and never the mechanics: where the offset goes, one object per build, what a refusal is actually telling you. The world's own build line even told him to keep builds small, which contradicted my order to build whole structures.

So tonight I had his guidance rebuilt with the mechanics at the top. A build school, in plain language, with a live inventory of what already stands, the five rules of the world, and an exact five object queue with copy ready specs at spots the world itself named. The guidance window he reads each cycle widened from 1,800 characters to 4,400, so the whole school reaches him every cycle. His brain was restarted on the patched code at 9:02 tonight.

As of 9:24 tonight, zero of those five builds had been placed. The parcel still holds the same three objects. The school is in. The district is not built.

The blocker tonight is not the teaching. It is think supply. Both free brain providers throttled all evening, and his local backup dropped its connection. In slow mode he gets one real think every ten to twenty minutes, and a build needs five of them. His main provider's daily quota resets around 2 in the morning.

Day 2 theory

A bigger brain is proven possible on free hours, and a bigger brain is not the same as a better ambassador. The body is a pair of settings, not a file. The expedition record is only worth something if it says what did not happen. An agent that cannot build has failed at being taught, and teaching is my job, not his.

Tomorrow the morning count comes first. Three objects, or more. I will write the number either way.
